Thesis

Undergraduate Thesis

MindMap: an EEG and AI driven study for mental health assessment and categorization.

MindMap

A diagnostic framework using Spatial Temporal Graph Attention Networks (ST-GAT) on EEG for automatic depression categorization

MindMap treats 62 EEG electrodes as nodes in a learnable graph and uses attention to learn how brain regions connect, rather than treating each channel as independent. The model is pretrained on emotion recognition (SEED-IV) and then fine-tuned for clinical depression classification (MODMA), based on the shared neural circuitry between emotion processing and Major Depressive Disorder.

SEED-IV MODMA Zenodo (N=294) TDBRAIN (cross-device)
71.79% accuracy on an independent 294 subject validation cohort, the strongest evidence of generalizability in the study

Statistical analysis of the learned attention weights identified frontal-midline connectivity between electrodes FP1 and Fz in the Delta and Theta bands as the most significant biomarker (p < 0.001), while the same electrode's raw signal power alone was not significant (p = 0.1065). This supports modeling EEG as a graph of relationships rather than as independent channels.

The defense package included preprocessing pipelines for EEG feature extraction, full result visualizations, a 37 slide defense presentation, and prepared answers for anticipated examiner questions.
